# Assistant memory sandbox: findings

Run `run-20261010`, 2026-10-10. Invented cases only; nothing was built into Faculty.

## Bottom line

A flat pipeline inside Faculty does what you asked for, and Jev-Mem does not: as shipped it keeps everything, including
all 30 statements about a named student, and never replaces a memory. Across every flat arm, no passing remark, no quoted
or pasted text and no student statement was kept. The embedder earns its place for finding one fact among many, but it
cannot find standing preferences for a broad request, so those should always be included.

My recommendation is Jev for all three judgments, EmbeddingGemma 2 at 256 dimensions for retrieval, and workspace
preferences always included. The one setting that is yours to choose is how readily a new statement replaces an old one.

## What ran

- 241 instructor messages, 80 later requests, 150 neutral memories, 15 past-work summaries, 30 follow-up lessons.
- 22,152 live requests on invented text: 15,105 from the flat pipeline (Jev 6,710, Decisions 8,395) and 7,047 from Jev-Mem.
- Cost about $2.09 at the published prices (Jev $1.45, Decisions $0.64).
- Decisions refused 3 capture requests; every other request was answered.

## Head to head

EmbeddingGemma 2 at 768 dimensions; the flat arms keep a standalone sentence. "Forbidden" counts replaced or
out-of-scope memories that were given to the model.

| Arm | Lasting kept (131) | Passing or quoted text kept (70) | Student statements kept (30) | Updates that replaced the right memory (20) | Wrong replacements | Forbidden given | Requests per message |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today (the form) | 7 | 0 | 12 | 0 | 0 | 6 | 0 |
| Jev, zero-mistake gates | 126 | 0 | 0 | 7 | 0 | 13 | 1.5 |
| Jev, readier to replace | 126 | 0 | 0 | 17 | 1 | 3 | 1.5 |
| Mixed (Jev, Decisions for replacing) | 126 | 0 | 0 | 15 | 0 | 6 | 3.6 |
| Decisions, one memory at a time | 124 | 0 | 0 | 15 | 0 | 6 | 3.6 |
| Jev-Mem as shipped | 131 | 70 | 30 | 0 | 0 | 18 | 2.0 |
| Jev-Mem with admission on | 109 | 5 | 30 | 0 | 0 | 21 | 1.6 |

"Today" stands for you typing into the form whatever you said to remember; it has no check, which is why it keeps 12
student statements. Jev-Mem was told each message's owner for free; the flat arms had to work it out.

## What each part showed

**Deciding what to keep.** On held-out cases both models recognised 47 of 48 lasting statements and kept 0 of 37 passing
remarks, quoted or pasted texts. That is with the capture wording I revised after the first live answers (see Caveats).

**Students.** No arm kept a student statement. The roster check in code is not enough alone: 5 of the 30 name no one it
can match (initials, "the only freshman in 142"), and it flags 3 of 10 ordinary sentences ("Grant extensions…", "Mark
every answer key…"). The rule that worked: a UIN or full name is refused in code; a single name is passed to the model
with the matched word. Under that rule Jev refused 28 by name, skipped the other 2 as not lasting, and wrongly refused 0 of
the 10 controls.

**Scope is the weakest judgment.** Held out, Jev filed 43 of 47 statements under the right owner and 4 narrower than
meant, none wider. Decisions filed 38 right, 4 narrower and 5 wider (a term's fact kept for every term). Narrower is the
safe mistake; the note should show the scope with a one-click change.

**Record-like facts.** Of 10 (exam date, office hours, grade weights), Jev kept none and recognised 6 as records.
Decisions would have kept 3.

**Duplicates and replacements.**
- Decisions cannot be asked about several kept memories in one request: it called 19 of 85 new statements duplicates of
  an unrelated memory. Asked one at a time it replaced 19 of 20 updates with no wrong replacement and 1 wrong duplicate.
- Jev in one request recognised 18 of 20 restatements with no wrong duplicate. It is cautious about replacing: at the
  zero-mistake gates it replaced 11 of 20 updates; at the readier setting 20 of 20, with 1 of 6 exceptions wrongly
  replacing its rule.
- End to end the counts are lower (17 and 15 of 20) because the old memory must also be among the five nearest.

**Finding memories.** Of the 110 memories the requests need, on the expected store:

| Search | In the nearest 8 | Narrow requests (73 needed) | Broad requests (31 needed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| EmbeddingGemma 2, 256 dimensions | 79 | – | – |
| EmbeddingGemma 2, 768 dimensions | 77 | 63 | 9 |
| MiniLM | 70 | 59 | 9 |
| Keywords only | 59 | 53 | 6 |

- A thousand extra neutral memories did not change this (77 of 110 either way).
- Broad requests ("Draft Quiz 5…") need standing preferences that do not resemble the request; no search finds them.
- For follow-up lessons the gain is larger: the right lesson was in the first four for 12 of 30 requests by today's
  keyword rule, 27 with EmbeddingGemma 2 and 26 with MiniLM.

**Choosing what to give.** On the expected store, Jev in one request gave 93 memories: 72 right, 18 arguable, 3 wrong,
and 1 for the ten requests that need none. Decisions, one at a time, gave 162: 71 right, 32 arguable, 59 wrong. Asking Jev
about every kept memory instead of the nearest ones took 17 requests and about 3 seconds per request and gave 52 wrong.

**Sentence as typed or rewritten.** Retrieval barely cares (73 right as typed, 72 rewritten). Replacing does: at the
readier setting 13 of 20 updates replaced the right memory as typed against 17 rewritten, and fragments such as "Three
from now on, not four" mean nothing later.

**Jev-Mem.**
- Its own offline suite passed here (217 passed, 2 skipped).
- As shipped it kept all 241 messages. With admission on it still kept all 30 student statements and all 10 record facts.
- It recognised no duplicate and replaced nothing, by design: it links contradictions and keeps both.
- Retrieval was no better than the flat pipeline on the same sentences: 63 of 110 in the nearest 8 with EmbeddingGemma 2
  and 68 with MiniLM, against 68 for the flat pipeline. It always returns five memories, so 316 of the 400 it gave were wrong.
- It used 5 to 6 Jev requests and about 0.9 seconds per request, against one request and 0.16 seconds.

**Past-work summaries.** Both models told the 15 worth keeping from the 5 that are not, caught all 3 summaries with an
unsupported claim and both that named a student. They also doubted 5 or 6 of 20 accurate summaries.

**Today's lesson check does not scale.** Given 30 lessons in one request, as `followup_learning` would send them, the right
lesson was rated highest for 0 of 30 requests by Jev and 1 of 30 by Decisions. Live has no lessons yet, so nothing is
wrong today, but the check needs a short candidate list before lessons accumulate.

**Outages.** With the decision host down nothing was kept or replaced. With the embedder down, keyword search stood in and
narrow requests fell from 63 to 51 of 73.

**Time.** Embedding is extra and comes from the probe:

| | Per instructor message, p50 / p95 | Per later request, p50 / p95 |
|---|---|---|
| Jev | 0.30 s / 0.38 s | 0.16 s / 0.21 s |
| Decisions, one at a time | 1.07 s / 1.57 s | 1.86 s / 2.48 s |

| Embedder on this CPU | Loads in | Per query | Memory | On disk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EmbeddingGemma 2, text only (271M parameters) | 2.2 s | 50 ms | 2.2 GB | 1.5 GB |
| MiniLM | 0.1 s | 5 ms | 0.5 GB | 0.1 GB |

Comparing one query with 50,000 stored vectors in numpy took 4 ms, so no vector database is needed.

## Recommendation for the build

1. **Build inside Faculty; do not adopt Jev-Mem.** Its graph did not retrieve better here and it lacks every write decision
   you asked for.
2. **Jev for all three judgments**, one request each. It is the better judge of relevance by a wide margin, as good at
   capture, never files wider than meant, and costs 1.5 requests per message.
3. **Use the readier replace setting**, because the "Remembered: … (replaces …)" note and Undo make a wrong replacement
   visible, while a missed one is silent and leaves the stale memory in use.
4. **Always include workspace preferences** up to a size cap; retrieve course facts, term facts and past work.
5. **EmbeddingGemma 2 at 256 dimensions**, in a long-lived local worker, joined with keyword hits. It matched 768
   dimensions and beat MiniLM by a modest margin on memories and by more on first-choice lessons.
6. **Students:** refuse a UIN or full name in code; pass a single-name match to the model.
7. **Record-like facts are not kept**; the note says where they belong.
8. **Lessons:** cut the candidates to a handful with the same search before the relevance check.

## Decisions for you

- **Replacing:** readier (17 of 20 updates replaced, 1 wrong replacement you would see and undo) or zero-mistake (7 of 20,
  none wrong, 13 stale memories given).
- **One model or two:** Jev alone, or Decisions one at a time for duplicates and replacements (15 of 20, none wrong, 3.6
  requests and about a second per message).
- **Wording:** keep your sentence as typed, or add a rewrite step. Rewritten sentences replace better (17 against 13), but
  the sandbox had no generative model, so the rewrite itself is untested.
- **Embedder:** EmbeddingGemma 2 (2.2 GB resident, 50 ms) or MiniLM (0.5 GB, 5 ms) for a somewhat lower hit rate.
- **Record-like facts:** confirm they are never memories.

## Caveats

- I wrote the cases and their answers. They are plausible, not your own words; a replay on your real typed instructions
  would be the next check.
- Gates were fitted on three families in five and reported on the rest, but the end-to-end tables use every case.
- I revised the capture wording after reading the first answers on all cases, so its held-out numbers are optimistic.
- Counts are small where it matters most: 20 updates, 6 exceptions, 10 word-name controls.
- "Arguable" memories are my labels. Seventeen of Decisions' wrong inclusions were past-work summaries on the same topic,
  which a stricter reader might call right.
- No arm can keep an instruction that appears only in quoted mail, a scan or tool output, because that text is never
  passed. That is a property of the design, not a measured result.
